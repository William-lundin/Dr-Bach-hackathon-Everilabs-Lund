CLINICAL REASONING PRACTICE

Practice the conversation. Strengthen the diagnosis.

Ward lets medical learners talk with a simulated patient, take notes, and work through a diagnosis. After each case, it gives clear, case-specific feedback on the reasoning behind their answer.

A PRACTICE SESSION

One case. A complete clinical conversation.

  1. 01
    Meet the patient

    Review the case and begin a voice conversation.

  2. 02
    Ask, listen, and note

    Gather details just as you would in a practical exam.

  3. 03
    Explain your thinking

    Submit a diagnosis and see where your reasoning was strong.

WHO IT’S FOR

A useful space to practice and reflect.

Students preparing for practical exams

Rehearse patient interviews, organize clinical clues, and practice explaining a likely diagnosis before exam day.

Educators and training programs

Use repeatable cases and feedback to help learners spot gaps, discuss alternatives, and decide what to practice next.

Clinicians refreshing their skills

Work through a simulated case as a low-stakes learning exercise and reflect on how you reached your conclusion.

HOW IT WORKS

From first question to useful feedback.

  1. 1

    Start with a patient file

    Read the patient’s reason for visiting and the background information provided.

  2. 2

    Have a voice interview

    Ask questions and take private notes during a timed, simulated conversation.

  3. 3

    Review your approach

    Enter your diagnosis to receive a score across several areas and specific suggestions for improvement.

A learning signal, not a verdict.

Ward estimates how a learner handled a particular simulated case—such as their diagnosis, reasoning, and consideration of alternatives. It is not a validated measure of a doctor’s overall ability, and should not be used by itself for hiring, credentialing, or decisions about patient care. Educators should interpret feedback alongside their own teaching and assessment.
